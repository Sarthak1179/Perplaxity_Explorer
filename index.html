What is Perplexity?

In natural language processing and information theory, Perplexity (often abbreviated as PPL) is a mathematical measurement of how well a probabilistic language model predicts a sample of text.

The Dice / Branching Factor Analogy

Intuition: Think of perplexity as the number of equally likely sides on a die the model has to roll at each word.

  • If a model has a perplexity of 10 on a sentence, it means that at each token, the model was as uncertain as choosing uniformly among 10 equally probable choices.
  • If the perplexity is 1.0, the model was 100% certain of every single word before reading it.

Why Lower is Better

Lower perplexity signifies that the model found the text natural, fluent, and predictable. Higher perplexity implies the model encountered surprising, improbable, or grammatically scrambled phrasing.

How Does Perplexity Work?

Calculating perplexity follows a strict six-stage mathematical pipeline:

  1. Tokenization: The text is split into subword token IDs. For DistilGPT-2, the sequence begins with prefix marker 50256 (<|endoftext|>).
  2. Forward Pass: The tokens are passed through transformer layers, producing raw unnormalized logit vectors of size 50,257 for every position.
  3. Logit Alignment: To evaluate token \(w_i\), we extract the logits produced at position \(i - 1\) (the context up to that point).
  4. Stable Softmax: We subtract \(\max(\text{logit})\) before exponentiating to prevent numerical overflow, yielding true probability \(P(w_i \mid w_{
  5. Inverse Probability (Surprisal): The surprise of each token is \(\frac{1}{P(w_i)}\). Less probable words yield massive surprisal values.
  6. Geometric Mean via Log-Sum: The sequence perplexity is the geometric mean of all surprisals:
Perplexity = exp( (1 / N) * ∑ -ln(P_i) )

Using logarithms guarantees that the product will never overflow or underflow floating-point precision.

Understanding Perplexity Scores

Typical Score Benchmarks:

  • PPL < 20 (Highly Expected): Common idioms, factual statements, or frequent clichés (e.g. "The capital of France is Paris").
  • PPL 20 – 80 (Fluent Natural Prose): Standard conversational English, technical documentation, or creative narrative sentences.
  • PPL 100 – 300+ (High Surprisal): Unconventional grammar, rare vocabulary, or surprising plot twists.
  • PPL > 500+ (Extreme Uncertainty): Scrambled word orders (e.g. "Python love I"), random strings, or foreign language tokens.

Why Word Order Matters

Because GPT-2 is a causal autoregressive model, each word's probability is strictly conditioned on all preceding words. Rearranging word order scrambles the causal chain, drastically reducing transition probabilities and spiking perplexity.
